# 8. Your Claim Binder

## One folder for the whole season

A claim binder is a single place where Social Security paperwork lives
for as long as the claim and then some: the letters, the confirmation
numbers, the call notes, the name-tagged worksheets. It fits in a
kitchen drawer when closed and in a morning when needed. Its superpower
is unglamorous and enormous: **nothing important has to be remembered
twice.**

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

## The shopping list (about fifteen dollars and ten calm minutes)

- One **1-inch view-binder** (three rings, front pocket works).
- About **25 sheet protectors** — documents stay put, nobody staples.
- **Six tab dividers** (the colored ones, if luck permits).
- A **permanent marker** for writing on tabs.
- A small **notepad**, or simply the printed worksheets from this
  guide (that's what they're for).

## The six tabs

| Tab | Name | What lives here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Contacts** | This chapter's phone table, plus your local office (found via the [locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator)) with its address and hours; the names of helpers and family allowed to know what's happening |
| 2 | **Identity & documents** | Birth certificate, marriage certificate, decree, proof of citizenship — each in its own protector; the photocopies you made before showing or sending originals; your SSN card stays **home**, never in a commuter bag |
| 3 | **Claim records** | Every letter SSA sends, in date order; the application summary and **confirmation number** printed from an online claim; the document list the application gave you; anything-mailed receipts |
| 4 | **Money & deposits** | Bank routing and account number — noted only on the direct-deposit worksheet, discreetly; benefit-award letters; any SSA-1099; the reminder that a direct-deposit change can take **30–60 days** and the old account stays open until money lands in the new one |
| 5 | **Notes & worksheets** | Printed [worksheets](worksheets/binder-inventory-worksheet.md): the claim timeline, the call-log, the grab-list checklist; your own plain-note pages in between |
| 6 | **Scams & safety** | The [scam chapter's checklist](07-avoiding-scams.md), the never-list, and any suspicious letter you kept (real cons still get filed) |

## The before-during-after ritual

**Before a claim:** tab 2 fill-in (grab-list), tab 1 readied (numbers
and hours), the [claim timeline worksheet](worksheets/claim-timeline-worksheet.md)
dated, one quiet evening, kettle optionally involved.

**During a claim:** every call earns a worksheet line (date, time,
name, subject, confirmation number, next step); every letter goes to
tab 3 the day it arrives — same-day filing is the whole trick, and it
takes under a minute when the protectors are already open.

**After a claim:** the binder keeps working — award letters, the
payment Wednesday that matches your record-holder's birthday band, any
review letters, tax-time SSA-1099s. After a few years it graduates to a
file box; the tab labels travel with it.

## The privacy rules, put gently

- **Your SSN card lives somewhere safe at home.** You rarely need it;
  a partially written note in tab 2 — enough to recognize the number,
  not enough for anyone else to use — is all daily life asks for. This
  guide asks for neither.
- **Don't email originals or photos of certificates** to anyone but
  SSA's own instructed channels — real requests for documents come
  with the application's own mailing instructions.
- **Shred** what you retire, and keep nothing with a full number in
  the open if someone else in the house shouldn't.
- **An offline copy counts**: a photographed set of pages on an
  encrypted drive at home is a kindness to future-you; cloud accounts
  are optional and this guide has no opinion on them.

## The low-energy mode (for tired months, honest weeks)

Fifteen minutes, once a month, one tab at a time:

- Month 1: contacts + identity.
- Month 2: claim records (sort by date, protectors open).
- Month 3: money + worksheets; read one SSA letter slowly.
- Repeat, guiltlessly, forever.

The binder never needs to be caught up all at once; it only needs to
exist. When a busy or a grieving week happens, the binder's job is to
say what page you were on, so you can put it down again in peace.

## What the binder gets you

- A two-week-delay-proof set of facts for [scam calls](07-avoiding-scams.md):
  "I have my confirmation number; I'll call back at the official
  number."
- A claim conversation where you hold the worksheet and SSA holds the
  rules — no memory gymnastics required.
- A surviving-spouse season where one tab says everything a kind
  relative needs to help.
