# 4. Phone, Field Office, or Online

## The three doors, viewed from the hallway

Every piece of Social Security paperwork travels through one of three
doors: the **phone**, the **field office**, and the **website**. None
of the doors is the "real" one; all of them reach the same friendly
kitchen in the back where claims are actually processed. You may pick
by mood, by mobility, by guitar-strumming toddler in the next room, or
by the kind of person you are when someone says "please hold."

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

## The comparison, honestly

| Door | What it's great for | What it is not great for | The small print |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Online (ssa.gov)** | Applying for retirement, spouse (once within 3 months of age 62 or older), Medicare-only, and disability; checking status; printing verification letters and SSA-1099s; many replacement cards (in most areas) | Survivors' claims (no online application exists); anything that needs an original document **looked at** | You will create a **my Social Security** account, which sets you up with one of two credential services — **Login.gov** or **ID.me** — and needs a valid email address |
| **Phone (1-800-772-1213)** | Survivors' claims; scheduling appointments; questions about your own records; everything, really — "our representatives can make an appointment for you to apply" | Anything needing you to **show** a paper (phone can talk, not look) | TTY: 1-800-325-0778. Automated services run 24 hours a day; representatives answer **weekdays, 8:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m., your local time** |
| **Field office** | Handing over originals; surviving a complex conversation in person; sign-language and interpreter help on the spot | Again, nothing dangerous — just travel, waiting rooms, and pamphlet chairs | Generally open the public 9:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m., Monday–Friday; "hours may vary slightly in certain locations," so let the [locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator) decide, not folklore |

## The phone path, call by call

**When to call, official advice:** publication after publication repeats
the same pattern — for quicker access to a representative, try calling
**early in the day (between 8 a.m. and 10 a.m. local time)** or later
in the day; SSA is "less busy later in the week (Wednesday to Friday)
and later in the month." If you are deaf or hard of hearing, the TTY
number covers the same hours. Free interpreter services are available
on request — publications note the line serves callers "in English,
Spanish, and other languages."

**Have at hand:** the claim you're calling about, your binder, and the
[call-notes worksheet](worksheets/phone-call-notes-worksheet.md) — plus
anything the [document grab-list](03-document-grab-list.md) said you
would need to answer questions, like marriage dates and places.

**The kind script**, for when you know what you need:

> "Hello — I'd like to [apply for / schedule an appointment about /
> ask a question about] [benefit]. Before we start, could I ask: what
> documents should I have ready, and is there anything I can do
> online instead of waiting to talk to someone?"

Three things to write down **during** the call, in the worksheet:

1. the **representative's name**, to thank politely and to reference;
2. any **confirmation number** the call generates;
3. the **next step** and its date ("you'll get an application packet
   within two weeks" earns a note and a paperclip).

## The field-office path, visit by visit

1. **Find your office** with the [official locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator).
2. **Schedule if you can** — "an appointment is not required, but if
   you call ahead and schedule one, it may reduce the time you spend
   waiting to apply" (Form SSA-10's exact sentences; the same offer is
   on the practice-wide appointment FAQ).
3. **Pack the grab-list** — originals or certified copies, per
   [chapter 3](03-document-grab-list.md).
4. **Arrive early**, budget the morning, and consider bringing a helper;
   interpreters are available on request.
5. **Log what happens** — names, papers you left, papers you kept, and
   any next step, in the call-notes worksheet (it works at desks too).

## The online path, click by click

1. Visit [ssa.gov/apply](https://www.ssa.gov/apply/) — answer a few
   quick questions, and the site provides "start a new application"
   (or "finish an existing application," if the first attempt ran out
   of tea).
2. **Create the account** — my Social Security sets you up with
   Login.gov or ID.me; you need a valid email address, and identity
   verification happens once at the beginning.
3. **Fill in the claim** — the site asks for the month you want
   benefits to begin (for retirement), bank routing and account numbers
   for direct deposit, and work history. The official checklist for
   the online Medicare/retirement/spouses application is a real PDF
   you can print — it is linked from the application itself.
4. **Submit and save** — electronic signature, then a **confirmation
   number** on the spot ("you will not be able to reenter the
   application after submission," so the confirmation number is the
   most important digit of your week).
5. **Review the documents list** that appears at the end of the online
   application, and follow its mailing instructions for
   any papers SSA asks to see.
6. **Print or save the application summary and receipt** — both go
   straight to [the binder](08-claim-binder.md).

The same account keeps working after the claim: checking application
status, replacing an SSA-1099, printing a benefit verification letter,
and changing direct-deposit details **for people who already receive
benefits**.

One patient note about direct deposit: SSA's own publications mention
these changes can take **30 to 60 days** to complete, and suggest you
do **not close** the old account until benefits have actually landed in
the new one. It's the kind of detail that saves a rent panic.

## Which door for whom

- **Screens are fine, tea is hot:** the online door, and congratulations —
  you just finished claim paperwork in one sitting.
- **You'd rather talk it through:** the phone door, ideally Wednesday
  through Friday, morning or evening.
- **Originals in hand, or the situation is knotty:** the field office,
  appointment preferred, helper welcome.
- **Anyone,** really: anyone door may call 1-800-772-1213 and simply ask
  which door fits their situation. Asking is always allowed.
