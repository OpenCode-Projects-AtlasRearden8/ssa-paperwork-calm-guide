# 9. Glossary — Plain Words for Social Security Paper

Every definition here is **vocabulary, not instruction** — knowing what
a word means is different from advice, which this guide never gives.
The official definitions live on ssa.gov and in SSA's own publications;
these are the friendly translations, each covered more gently in its
home chapter.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 800-number (1-800-772-1213) | Social Security's national toll-free line; automated services 24 hours a day, representatives weekdays 8:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m. local time |
| 800-number quiet hours | Official advice for reaching a representative faster: try early (8–10 a.m. local), later in the day, later in the week (Wednesday–Friday), and later in the month |
| Advance designation | Choosing your preferred representative payee in advance, in case you ever can't manage benefits yourself |
| Application status | The progress of a filed claim — checkable free in your my Social Security account |
| Appointment (in-person) | A reserved slot at your field office; not required, but may "reduce the time you spend waiting to apply" |
| Appointment (telephone) | SSA takes your claim **over the phone** at an agreed time — you'll get a call from SSA, which is one of the legitimate reasons SSA calls you |
| Certified copy | A photocopy stamped as genuine by the agency that issued the original — one of only two forms (with originals) SSA accepts for most vital documents |
| Claim | The paperwork that opens a benefit door; a claim "on your record" or "on someone else's record" tells you whose work years back it |
| COLA | Cost-of-living adjustment — the yearly benefit increase SSA announces each year; note SSA never asks for banking or personal details "to give you a COLA" |
| Confirmation number | The number an online application gives you at submission — the most important digit of your claim week |
| Credits (work credits) | Notches earned by working and paying Social Security taxes; four per year at most; 40 to open the retirement door; the dollar-per-credit is re-set each year on ssa.gov |
| Direct deposit | How benefits arrive in your bank account; changes can take 30–60 days, and SSA suggests not closing the old account until money lands in the new one |
| Disability Starter Kit | SSA's official preparation kit for disability claims — adults' and children's versions — listing what the claim will ask |
| Divorced spouse's benefit | Benefits a former spouse may draw on the worker's record: marriage of at least 10 years, age 62-plus, unmarried, with the own-benefit comparison SSA checks |
| DD-214 | The official discharge paper for military service before 1968 claims — photocopies acceptable |
| Earn limit | A yearly earnings cutoff that can reduce benefits claimed before full retirement age; SSA publishes the amounts in *How Work Affects Your Benefits* |
| Enrollment month | The month you choose in a retirement application for benefits to start; your first payment arrives the month after it |
| Extra Help | The official program lowering Medicare prescription-drug costs for people with limited income and resources — applied for through SSA |
| Family maximum | SSA's ceiling on what a family as a whole can draw from one record — "generally about 150% to 180%" of the worker's amount |
| Field office | Your local SSA office; open to the public generally 9:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Monday–Friday, with slight local variation |
| Full retirement age (FRA) | The age of unreduced retirement benefits, 66 to 67 by birth year (67 for 1960 and later); survivors' claims have their own FRA table (66 for 1945–1956 births) |
| Government Pension Offset (GPO) | The old rule that reduced spouse's/survivor's benefits for public pensions — **ended by the Social Security Fairness Act**; inapplicable to benefits payable January 2024 and later |
| Impersonation scam | A caller or letter pretending to be SSA; the never-list in [chapter 7](07-avoiding-scams.md) is the counter-spell |
| Investigator (SSA OIG) | The Office of the Inspector General — SSA's law-enforcement arm, and the place scam reports go (oig.ssa.gov) |
| Lump-sum death payment | The one-time **$255** payment to a qualifying spouse or child, applied for within 2 years of the worker's death |
| Marriage certificate | THE document for spouse and survivor claims — originals or certified copies only |
| Medicare-only application | SSA's short online sign-up for Medicare for people within 3 months of turning 65 who aren't ready to claim monthly benefits — under ten minutes, no forms to sign, usually no documentation required |
| my Social Security | Your free online account (built on Login.gov or ID.me): statements, estimates, status, replacement cards, verification letters |
| Online claim | An application started and submitted at ssa.gov/apply — retirement, spouse (within 3 months of 62), Medicare-only, disability; **not** survivors |
| Original document | The true paper itself; most SSA documents must be seen this way (or certified), and SSA returns them |
| Pay.gov | One of SSA's official electronic payment routes for amounts actually owed — gift cards are never on that list |
| Payment Wednesday | The monthly payment day, set by the record-holder's birthday band: 1st–10th → 2nd Wednesday; 11th–20th → 3rd; 21st–31st → 4th; SSI pays the 1st, both on the 3rd |
| Photocopy rule | W-2s, self-employment tax returns, and medical documents photocopy happily; birth certificates and friends do not |
| Representatives (SSA) | The people behind 1-800-772-1213; authorized to schedule appointments, check records, and explain options in writing upon request |
| Representative payee | Someone SSA appoints to manage benefits for a person who can't; power of attorney or guardianship does not automatically qualify anybody |
| Social Security Statement | Your free official earnings-and-estimates report, in your my Social Security account — the source of your real numbers |
| SSDI | Social Security Disability Insurance — disability benefits on your own work record, with a five-month waiting period (none for ALS approved on/after July 23, 2020) |
| SSI | Supplemental Security Income — the separate, need-based program for people with limited income and resources; administered by SSA but not paid from Social Security taxes |
| Spoofing | Fake caller ID — the phone lies about who is calling; dialed-by-you numbers are the antidote |
| Survivors benefits | Benefits on a deceased worker's record: widow(er) 100% at (survivor) FRA, 71–99% from 60, caretakers and children at 75%, disabled widow(er) as early as 50 |
| TTY (1-800-325-0778) | The text-telephone version of the national line, same weekday hours |
| Verification letter | The printable letter from my Social Security proving your benefits to landlords, agencies, and worthy skeptics |
| WEP | Windfall Elimination Provision — the old public-pension offset for *your own* benefits; also **ended**, effective for benefits payable January 2024 and later |
| Widow's/widower's benefit | Survivor benefits for a surviving spouse — from age 60 (50 if disabled), on the deceased worker's record |

## Words that sound scarier than they are

- **"Suspension"** of a Social Security number — not a thing that
  happens; anyone threatening it is selling a costume.
- **"Overpayment"** letters — ordinary paperwork asking for money
  back, with appeal rights described on paper; read slowly, reply in
  writing, keep a copy.
- **"Representative"** — here meaning a real SSA employee on the
  phone; also used elsewhere for hired advocates. When SSA says
  "representative," it's a person who wants to help, not a thing to
  worry about.
