# 2. Which Appointment You Likely Need

## First, the relief: you may not need an appointment at all

Here is the quiet secret that makes this chapter worth reading: most
Social Security business starts **online**, with no appointment, no
waiting room, and no travel. SSA's own guidance is that the most
convenient way to do business is at [ssa.gov](https://www.ssa.gov/) —
you can apply for retirement, spouse, Medicare, and disability benefits,
request a replacement Social Security card (in most areas), check an
application's status, and print documents from a free **my Social
Security** account. The appointment chapter exists only for the parts
that genuinely need a person.

So the real question this chapter answers is not "which appointment do I
need?" but "**do I need one — and if so, which kind?**"

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

## The three kinds of contact

| Kind | What it feels like | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No appointment (online)** | Ten quiet minutes at your own kitchen table, your own tea, your own cat | Retirement, spouse (once within 3 months of age 62 or older), Medicare-only, disability applications; statements, verification letters, replacement cards, application status |
| **Phone** | A call you place to 1-800-772-1213 yourself — or an appointment where **SSA calls you at an agreed time** | Survivors' claims (no online path), scheduling anything, questions about your own records, if screens aren't your friend |
| **In-person (field office)** | A visit to your local office; appointments by phone ahead of time "may reduce the time you spend waiting," in SSA's words | Walking in with original documents, complex situations, or if you simply want a human handshake |

## The "what do I need → what to do" table

Read down the first column until you find your sentence:

| Your sentence | The likely path |
| --- | --- |
| "I want to claim **retirement** benefits." | Online at [ssa.gov/apply](https://www.ssa.gov/apply/) (you will create or sign in to a my Social Security account); or call 1-800-772-1213; or visit your office — *"call first to make an appointment"*, per SSA's retirement FAQ |
| "I want to claim **spouse or divorced spouse** benefits." | Online once you are within 3 months of age 62 or older (Form SSA-2's rules); otherwise by phone or at the office — appointment optional but welcome |
| "Someone close to me has **died**." | Report the death by phone or office; **survivors' benefits cannot be applied for online** — details in [Survivor Claims, Patiently](06-survivor-claims.md) |
| "I want to apply for **disability**." | Online (with the official Disability Starter Kit), or by calling 1-800-772-1213 — "our representatives can make an appointment for you to apply" |
| "I just need **Medicare**, not my retirement yet." | The Medicare-only online application — official description: under ten minutes, no forms to sign, usually no documentation required |
| "I want to **check on an application I started**." | Your my Social Security account, no appointment needed |
| "I lost my **card** / need a **verification letter** / need a **new SSA-1099**." | Your my Social Security account; a replacement card is possible online in most areas |
| "I have **original documents** (certificate, decree) to hand over." | Field office, usually by appointment — or mailing instructions if you claimed online (the application tells you where to send them) |
| "My situation is **unusual** and I want to talk to a person about it." | Phone, or an in-person appointment — schedule either one by calling 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778) or contacting your local office |

## Scheduling an appointment, officially

Per SSA's appointment FAQ, you can **schedule, reschedule, or cancel**
an appointment by calling **1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778),
Monday through Friday between 8:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. local time**, or
by contacting your local Social Security office. When you call, you may
say which kind you would like — SSA offers to take claims **over the
telephone** at an agreed time, or **in person**. Appointment times are
worth grabbing for survivors' claims, for handing over originals, and
for any situation where a guaranteed half-hour of a specialist's
attention sounds kinder than a waiting room.

## A note of calm for field-office appointments

The [official locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator) finds your office
and its hours. Hours are generally 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday
through Friday, though they "may vary slightly in certain locations,"
in SSA's own words — so let the locator, not folklore, decide when you
go. Arrive a little early with the documents your
[grab-list](03-document-grab-list.md) told you to bring, and remember
the samples of kindness: you can bring a helper, ask for an
interpreter, and say "I'd like to check my paperwork with someone
before I sign anything." All three are normal requests.

## When in doubt, the gentlest first step

You may want to try this: **make the call that needs nothing.**
1-800-772-1213 asked *by you*, on a quiet weekday morning, to ask one
ordinary question like "For a survivors claim, should I call you back
to make an appointment, or walk in?" Nobody is judging the question.
The phone number lives in [your binder](08-claim-binder.md), the
[call-notes worksheet](worksheets/phone-call-notes-worksheet.md) keeps
the answer, and [Chapter 4](04-phone-field-online.md) walks the whole
call politely.
