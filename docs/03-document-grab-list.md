# 3. The Document Grab-List

## The kindest rule first: don't wait for the folder to be perfect

SSA says this repeatedly and it deserves to be underlined everywhere:
**Do not delay applying because you do not have all the documents.** The
official applications state it plainly — visitors may "provide the
missing documents later," and local offices can often verify records
with the state at no cost to you. So this grab-list is not a wall
between you and a claim. It is a picnic basket: pack what you can, and
let SSA help carry the rest.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

## The originals protocol (short and important)

| Rule | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Original or certified copy | For most documents, SSA must see the originals or copies certified by the agency that issued them — **photocopies and notarized copies are not accepted** for things like birth certificates |
| The photocopy exception | W-2 forms, self-employment tax returns, and medical documents may be **photocopies** |
| You get things back | SSA returns the documents you show or send |
| Two years to be found? | If SSA's records already hold proof of age, citizenship, or lawful alien status from an earlier claim (Medicare, disability, SSI), you do **not** need to provide it again |

## Everyone's grab-list (any claim)

- Your **Social Security number** — the card, or simply a record of the
  number.
- Your **birth certificate** — original, or a copy certified by the
  issuing agency. (Other proof of birth can work if the certificate is
  truly unavailable; ask SSA before improvising.)
- If you were **not born in the U.S.**: proof of U.S. citizenship or
  lawful alien status. SSA warns that it cannot accept expired
  documents.
- **U.S. military discharge papers** (for example, a DD-214) if you had
  military service **before 1968** — a photocopy is acceptable.
- A copy of your **W-2 form(s) and/or self-employment tax return** for
  last year — photocopies are acceptable.
- **Direct deposit details** — your bank's routing number and your
  account number, so benefits can arrive on their own.

Official pattern: these lines appear in SSA's retirement-application
pages (Form SSA-1 and the retirement planner's
[documents page](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/applying5.html)),
and the same spine repeats for every claim type below.

## Plus, for claims on a living spouse's record (spouse / divorced spouse)

Per Form SSA-2's official list:

- **Marriage certificate.**
- **Final divorce decree**, if applying as a divorced spouse.
- The everyone's list above (your birth certificate, and so on).

One more thing to gather that is a *number*, not a paper: dates and
places of your marriages and divorces, and each spouse's name and
Social Security number — the SSA-2's question list includes these, and
having them written down makes the conversation shorter.

## Plus, for disability claims

- The **Disability Starter Kit** — SSA publishes official kits for
  adults and for children under 18, with the worksheets SSA will ask
  about (your conditions, your doctors, your work history).
- The two supporting forms SSA names for disability claims: the
  **SSA-3368** (Adult Disability Report) and the **SSA-827**
  (authorization to disclose medical information).
- Medical documents — photocopies acceptable — covering the condition,
  the treatment, and the providers. The Starter Kit tells you exactly
  what to list.

## Plus, for survivors' claims

From SSA Form SSA-10 and the official
*Survivors Benefits* publication:

- **Proof of the worker's death** — from the funeral home or the death
  certificate.
- The **worker's Social Security number** as well as yours.
- Your **birth certificate**.
- Your **marriage certificate**, if you are the widow or widower.
- **Divorce papers**, if applying as a surviving divorced spouse.
- **Dependent children's Social Security numbers** (if available) and
  birth certificates.
- The worker's most recent **W-2 forms or federal self-employment tax
  return**.
- **Your bank's routing and account numbers** for direct deposit.

And the survivors' edition of the kind rule: people are asked *not* to
delay contacting Social Security because documentation is missing —
"we'll help you get what you need."

## A gentle word about what to keep safe

Some of these papers earn a permanent tab in your
[claim binder](08-claim-binder.md): the marriage certificate, the death
certificate, the court decree. While you are gathering, consider
photocopying everything *for yourself* before any original leaves your
hands — SSA returns originals, but a copy in your own folder means you
never lose sleep waiting for one.

The [Document Grab-List Checklist](worksheets/document-grab-list-checklist.md)
turns this chapter into checkboxes with blank lines for paperwork that
lives elsewhere — a relative's filing cabinet, a safe-deposit box —
because that is usually where half of it hides.
