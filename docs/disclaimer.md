# The Education-Only Promise

## What this guide is

The SSA Paperwork Calm Guide explains **paper**. It explains what
Social Security is, which appointment you likely need, which documents
wait in which folder, how the phone and office and website doors work,
how spouse and survivor claims take shape, how impostors sound, and how
one binder holds the season together — explained like a friend would:
plain English, step by step, never condescending.

## What this guide is not, in one set of capital letters

**THIS GUIDE EXPLAINS PAPERWORK AND GIVES NO ADVICE.** Indeed, the
capital letters want to be specific:

- It does not and will not tell you **when to claim**, **whether to
  claim**, or **which benefit** is worth more for your family.
- It does not give **legal advice** — appeals and complex denials
  deserve a professional, and SSA regulates representatives' fees
  precisely so you can check.
- It does not give **financial advice** — not about taxes on benefits,
  not about annuities, not about anything an investment professional
  should weigh in on.
- It does not give **medical advice** — disability decisions belong to
  SSA's process and your doctors, not to a book.
- It **never asks for your personal information**, because it can't:
  it's a folder of Markdown files on your own computer. If a page, a
  pop-up, or a "helper" ever asks you for a Social Security number
  "so this guide can process something," it is not this guide.

## Where real decisions actually happen

| The question | Who answers it |
| --- | --- |
| "When should I claim?" | You — informed by your own [Statement](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/) and, if you wish, a fee-regulated financial professional |
| "Can I get survivor or spouse benefits now?" | SSA itself — at 1-800-772-1213, online, or at your field office |
| "Is my paperwork missing something?" | SSA's document lists, and a real representative's review |
| "Is this letter a scam?" | The [never-list](07-avoiding-scams.md), then oig.ssa.gov when in doubt |
| "Where do my real numbers come from?" | Your own letters and Statement — never from this guide |

## The one thing worth repeating

SSA's representatives are paid — by the government, at official
hours — to explain your options to you, including "so you can decide
which would be best for you." A book that pretends to do that job for
you is overstepping. This one would rather leave you fluent, unhurried,
and holding a binder.

## About kindness

The "verbs from kindness" style of this guide is a deliberate stance,
not decoration: paperwork manuals are read during weddings, retirements,
stroke recoveries, and funerals. *You may*, *when you're ready*, *this
can wait* are chosen words — because for the person holding the folder,
they are true.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*
