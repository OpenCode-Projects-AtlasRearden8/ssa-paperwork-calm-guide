# 1. Social Security in One Picture

## What Social Security is, in one paragraph

Social Security is a **federal program** run by the Social Security
Administration (SSA) — the office behind the letters and the
1-800-772-1213 number. When you work and pay Social Security taxes,
your earnings build a *record*. That record eventually opens doors:
monthly benefits for **you** (retirement, or disability if a serious
condition interrupts), benefits for your **family** while the record's
worker is retired or disabled, and — hardest to say out loud —
benefits for your family **after a worker dies** (survivors). SSA also
runs two neighbors of the program: **SSI**, a separate need-based
program for people with limited income and resources, and the
**Medicare sign-up desk**, which is why a lot of people first meet
Social Security right before age 65.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

```
  ...and separately across the street:

        and resources; SSA administers it, but it is
        not paid from Social Security taxes)

  paperwork manual - see medicare-decoded-guide).
```

Read the picture left to right and it says this: **one record, many
doors**, and each door has its own paperwork season.

- **Retirement** — the door most people picture. Benefits on your own
  record, claiming from your 60s onward, reduced if you claim early.
- **Disability** (SSDI) — benefits on your own record when a severe
  condition prevents substantial work and is expected to last at least
  a year or end in death. SSA does not pay for partial or short-term
  disability.
- **Family benefits** — while a worker's claim is open, a spouse,
  divorced spouse, or unmarried child may ride on the same record.
- **Survivors** — when a worker dies, the record keeps working for the
  people left behind. [Chapter 6](06-survivor-claims.md) walks this one
  gently, step by step.

## The numbers SSA actually uses (all official, all sourced)

| Concept | The official shape | Where it's published |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work credit | You earn credits from working and paying Social Security taxes | [ssa.gov — credits FAQ](https://www.ssa.gov/faqs/en/questions/KA-02459.html) |
| Credits per year | Maximum of four in a year | Same credits FAQ |
| Credits for retirement | 40 (about ten years) for anyone born 1929 or later | [How You Earn Credits, SSA publication](https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10072.pdf) |
| Credit ceiling | No one needs more than 40 credits for any benefit | Credits FAQ |
| Dollar per credit | Set yearly as wages rise — **in 2026, one credit per $1,890 earned** | Credits FAQ and [How You Earn Credits](https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10072.pdf) |
| Benefit amount | Based on your highest **35** years of earnings; missing years count as zeros | [SSA my-Social-Security fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/assets/materials/EN-05-10703.pdf) |
| Credits never expire | They stay on your record even if you stop working for a while | How You Earn Credits |

Two things are worth saying out loud, because guides elsewhere mumble
them: the dollar amount per credit **changes every year**, so this guide
prints only the officially labeled figure and leaves the future years
to ssa.gov; and credit *count* is separate from credit *size* — you can
earn all four credits in one busy month and they still simply count.

## The cast of characters

| Who | What they actually do |
| --- | --- |
| **SSA** | The Social Security Administration. Runs the claims, the payments, the SSI program, the Medicare sign-up desk, the field offices, and the 800-number. It is not a bond-selling operation and it is not a lawyer's office. |
| **Your record** | The unseen main character. All the claims in this guide, including a spouse's or survivor's, ultimately lean on somebody's record of credits. |
| **my Social Security (your account)** | The free online account at [ssa.gov/myaccount](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/): your Social Security Statement, benefit estimates, a printable benefit verification letter, and more. |
| **1-800-772-1213** | The national toll-free line (TTY 1-800-325-0778). Automated services run 24 hours; a representative answers on weekdays between 8:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m., local time. |
| **Your field office** | The local office, found via the [official locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator). Open to the public generally 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Monday–Friday (hours vary slightly by location). |
| **Medicare & CMS** | A different agency and program. SSA handles the *sign-up*; Medicare handles the insurance itself — covered patiently in *medicare-decoded-guide*. |
| **OIG** | SSA's Office of the Inspector General — the law-enforcement arm that collects scam reports at [oig.ssa.gov](https://oig.ssa.gov/). You will meet it in [the scams chapter](07-avoiding-scams.md). |
| **Your claim binder** | The hero of this guide. See [Your Claim Binder](08-claim-binder.md). |

## Five words, patient versions

| Word | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Credit | A notch on a work record; at most four notches a year; the official yearly dollar amount is on ssa.gov |
| Claim | The paperwork that opens a benefit door — applying |
| Statement | Your free official report of earnings and benefit estimates, in your my Social Security account |
| Full retirement age (FRA) | The age when unreduced retirement benefits are yours; it ranges from 66 to 67 depending on birth year, and your Statement tells you yours |
| Direct deposit | The way benefits arrive in your bank account — routing number and account number, kept safe in your binder |
