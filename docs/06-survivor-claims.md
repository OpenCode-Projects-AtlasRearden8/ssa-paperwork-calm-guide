# 6. Survivor Claims, Patiently

## Before anything else

Someone you love has died, and someone has told you to call Social
Security. We are very sorry. This chapter is written slow on purpose,
because paperwork during grief needs shorter steps, not faster ones.
You are allowed to read only the first heading today, put the kettle
on, and come back tomorrow; the chapter will wait.

*Education and paperwork help only — never medical, legal, financial, or
benefits advice.*

## Step one: the death gets reported (often already done)

Official guidance is kind here: **funeral homes generally tell SSA when
someone dies** — most families don't need to report a death at all. If
no funeral home was involved, or they didn't report it, family members
should call SSA and provide the person's **name, Social Security
number, date of birth, and date of death** — at 1-800-772-1213 (TTY
1-800-325-0778). When SSA asks, answer gently; none of this needs a
flowing conversation, and phone calls can be made by anyone in the
family.

## Step two: know that there may be benefits on that record

When a worker dies, the record the worker built keeps working — SSA
pays **survivors benefits** based on the earnings of the person who
died. In most typical claims, per SSA's official publications:

| Who | What SSA generally pays |
| --- | --- |
| Widows, widowers — full retirement age or older | **100%** of the worker's basic benefit amount |
| Widows/widowers age 60 up to full retirement age | **71% to 99%** |
| Widow/widower **of any age** caring for the worker's child younger than 16 | **75%** |
| Each eligible child (under 18, or 18–19 in elementary/secondary school) | **75%** |
| A disabled widow or widower, starting as early as age 50 | Reduced from the full amount; disability is SSA's call to make |

Two neighboring facts, gently:

- The **full retirement age for survivors** differs from the FRA for
  retirement benefits: it's **66 for people born 1945 through 1956**,
  rises gradually for birth years 1957 through 1962, and is **67 for
  anyone born 1962 or later**. That's why the ages in the table don't
  behave like the retirement tables in other guides.
- There is also a **one-time $255 lump-sum death payment** for a
  qualifying spouse or child, and SSA asks claimants to apply for it
  **within 2 years** of the death. It has kept its same modest amount
  for many years — SSA's current figure is the one to trust.

## Step three: how applying actually happens

Survivors claims have **no online door**: SSA states plainly, "You
cannot apply for survivors benefits online." Two routes:

1. **Call 1-800-772-1213** (TTY 1-800-325-0778), weekdays 8 a.m. to
   7 p.m., local time, or
2. **Visit your local field office** (find it with the
   [locator](https://www.ssa.gov/locator); hours generally
   9:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m., Monday–Friday).

SSA's own words to make the trip unscary: *"An appointment is not
required, but if you call ahead and schedule one, it may reduce the
time you spend waiting to apply."* Bring the
[documents list](03-document-grab-list.md) from chapter 3 (proof of
death, certificates, the worker's latest W-2 or self-employment tax
return, bank details for direct deposit) — and again, if something is
missing, **file anyway**: "we'll help you get what you need."

**Apply promptly.** SSA's publication points out gently: for some
claims, benefits are paid **from the time you apply**, not from the
time the worker died — so waiting costs money the delay can't
reimburse.

## Step four: if you were already receiving benefits

- If you receive spouse's benefits on the deceased's record, SSA
  **changes payments automatically** to survivor benefits when you
  report the death. If more information is needed, SSA contacts you.
- If you receive benefits **on your own record**, you must
  **complete an application** for survivor benefits — SSA checks
  whether more is owed and pays "a combination" equal to the higher
  amount, not both at once.

## Step five: the questions families actually ask

- **"I would rather get my own benefit instead of the survivor's."**
  You cannot receive both on one dollar; the *combination* equals the
  higher. SSA's publication invites the exact fairness-seeking phrasing:
  tell SSA you'd like a representative "to explain the options so you
  can decide which would be best."
- **"I've remarried."** Per SSA's official guidance: if you remarry
  **before age 60** (or 50 if disabled), you can't receive widow's or
  widower's benefits while that marriage lasts; if you remarry **at age
  60 or older** (or 50 or older if disabled), you may continue to
  receive them; and if the new spouse is also a Social Security
  beneficiary, you should look at the spouse benefit that would be
  larger — but only one record pays at a time.
- **"I received a payment after my spouse died."** Benefits are not
  payable **for the month the person died**. Payments for the prior
  month may still arrive; SSA will help you return or adjust anything
  needing adjustment — no one is in trouble; it is simply the rule.
- **"My family reads the payment dates differently than everyone
  else."** Monthly benefits are paid on a **Wednesday**, chosen by the
  worker on whose record the claim sits: birthdays on the 1st–10th pay
  the second Wednesday, the 11th–20th the third, and 21st–end the
  fourth Wednesday of each month. (SSI pays the 1st; people receiving
  both SSI and Social Security get both on the 3rd.) Benefits are
  paid in the month after they are due.
- **"Someone else manages the money."** When a beneficiary can't manage
  funds, SSA can appoint a **representative payee** — and SSA's
  publications stress that "legal guardianship" or "power of attorney"
  do **not** automatically qualify someone. Families may also choose
  one in advance using SSA's **advance designation** option. Both
  kindnesses happen through SSA, not through paperwork alone.

## What this chapter is not

It is not a rulebook for your family's situation, and it is not advice
about benefit choices. It exists so you can read calmly and then say
kind, clear things to SSA like "I'm calling about my spouse of thirty
years; may I ask what benefits may be available on their record?" The
conversation will be gentler now, and you'll have your
[claim binder](08-claim-binder.md) to put the answers in.
